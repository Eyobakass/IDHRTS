# 🔍 How the System Works Right Now in Ethiopia
### Rental Contracts & Rental Income Tax — Addis Ababa (2024–2026)
*Research based on Ethiopian proclamations, ERCA/MOR portals, PwC Ethiopia, EY Ethiopia, and academic sources*

---

## PART 1 — Rental Contract Signing (How it Works Today)

---

### The Laws That Govern It

| Law | What it Does |
|-----|-------------|
| Ethiopian Civil Code (1960) | The foundational lease law — defines landlord/tenant rights, rent setting, termination |
| **Proclamation No. 1320/2024** | THE main current law — mandatory written contracts, mandatory registration, rent controls |
| Addis Ababa Directive No. 7/2024 | Implements Proc. 1320/2024 at woreda (district) level |
| Directive No. 184/2025 | Further updated rules for Addis Ababa rental administration |

---

### Step-by-Step: How a Rental Contract Works Right Now

**Step 1 — Property Search**
- Done through online platforms (Jiji.com.et, BetDelala, Home Addis, Ethiobetoch), real estate agents, or word-of-mouth
- No government listing portal exists — everything is informal
- Physical inspection is critical (electricity, water, structural condition)

**Step 2 — Negotiation**
- Landlord and tenant agree on rent freely for first-time rentals
- **Advance payment is legally capped at 2 months' rent** (Proc. 1320/2024)
- For renewals: rent increases capped at government-announced annual ceiling (e.g., 11.5% for 2026/27)
- **Minimum lease term: 2 years**

**Step 3 — Drafting and Signing the Contract**
- A written contract is **legally mandatory** — verbal agreements have no legal effect under current law
- The Addis Ababa Housing Development and Administration Bureau provides a **standardized "Model Residential Rent Agreement"** template
- Contract must include: names, property address, rent amount, duration, payment schedule, condition of house

**Step 4 — Registration at the Woreda Housing Office (Mandatory within 30 days)**
- Both landlord and tenant must physically go to the **Woreda (District) Housing Development and Administration Office**
- Submit both a hard copy and a soft copy of the signed contract
- **If not registered within 30 days → the contract is legally void (no legal standing in court)**

**Step 5 — Move In + Payment**
- Rent payments must be made through **formal banking channels or digital payment systems** (Telebirr, bank transfer)
- Cash-only arrangements are not legally recognized under the new rules

---

### Which Government Office Handles Registration?

| Level | Office | Role |
|-------|--------|------|
| District (front-line) | **Woreda Housing Development and Administration Office** | Actual contract registration |
| City | Addis Ababa Housing Development and Administration Bureau | Policy, model contracts, central database |
| Federal | Ministry of Urban and Infrastructure | National policy |
| Courts | 3-tier system: Woreda → Grievance Committee → Court | Dispute resolution |

> **Note:** The Kebele is NOT the registration office — it only handles ID and residence verification

---

### Documents Required for Registration

| Party | Documents |
|-------|-----------|
| Landlord | Signed contract (hard + soft copy), valid ID, **Title Deed (proof of property ownership)** |
| Tenant | Signed contract, valid ID |
| Agent (if used) | Notarized Power of Attorney |

---

### Is the Current System Digital or Paper?

**Hybrid — transitioning to digital, but still mostly manual:**
- Contracts submitted in BOTH hard copy and soft copy at the Woreda office
- Addis Ababa has built a central digital database — approximately **500,000 residential properties** registered as of mid-2026
- But: **no fully self-service online portal for registration yet** — you still have to physically go to the Woreda office for authentication
- Digital systems are maturing but walk-in remains the primary process

---

### Penalty for Not Registering

| Violation | Consequence |
|-----------|------------|
| Unregistered contract | **Legally void — no court protection** |
| Non-registration fine | **Up to 3 months' rent** |
| Property vacant >6 months | Owner taxed on **imputed rental income** as if rented |
| Side contracts outside the system | Void + loss of all legal protections |

## PART 2 — Rental Income Tax (How it Works Today)

---

### The Laws That Govern It

| Law | What it Does |
|-----|-------------|
| **Income Tax Proclamation No. 979/2016** | Primary federal income tax law — Schedule B = rental income |
| Tax Administration Proclamation No. 983/2016 | How tax is administered, penalties, interest on arrears |
| **Income Tax Amendment Proclamation No. 1395/2025** | Updated tax brackets — effective July 7, 2025 |
| Proclamation No. 1320/2024 | Also impacts tax (imputed income, mandatory banking trail) |

---

### Tax Brackets — Schedule B (Rental Income)

> These are the CURRENT brackets since July 2025 (Proclamation 1395/2025):

| Annual Taxable Rental Income (ETB) | Tax Rate |
|-------------------------------------|----------|
| 0 – 24,000 | **0% (Exempt)** |
| 24,001 – 48,000 | 15% |
| 48,001 – 84,000 | 20% |
| 84,001 – 120,000 | 25% |
| 120,001 – 168,000 | 30% |
| Over 168,000 | **35%** |

> For companies/PLCs: flat **30%** on taxable rental income

---

### How Taxable Income Is Calculated

**Taxable Income = Gross Rent MINUS Allowable Deductions**

**For most individual landlords (no formal books):**
- Standard deduction = **20% of gross rent** (covers maintenance, repairs, depreciation)
- Also deduct: land/building taxes paid

**Worked Example:**
```
Landlord earns ETB 60,000/year gross rent
Standard deduction: 20% × 60,000 = ETB 12,000
Taxable income: 60,000 - 12,000 = ETB 48,000

Tax calculation (new brackets):
  0% on first 24,000        = ETB 0
  15% on next 24,000        = ETB 3,600
  Total tax = ETB 3,600

Effective tax rate: ~6% of gross rent
```

---

### Who Collects the Tax?

| Collector | Who They Tax |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Addis Ababa City Revenue Bureau** | Most individual landlords (Category C taxpayers) |
| Ministry of Revenues (MOR/ERCA) | Large taxpayers (annual turnover >ETB 1,000,000) |
| Kebele | NOT a tax collector — only ID/residence administration |

**Taxpayer Categories:**
- **Category C** (most individual landlords): Annual income under ETB 500,000 → file at local tax center
- **Category B**: ETB 500,001–1,000,000 → city revenue bureau
- **Category A**: Over ETB 1,000,000 → Ministry of Revenues

---

### How Does a Landlord Pay Today? (Step by Step)

**Option A — Online via TASS (the government's e-tax portal):**
1. Go to https://tass.mor.gov.et
2. Register for an account and get a TIN (Tax Identification Number)
3. Navigate to Schedule B → enter rental income → submit declaration
4. Download payment slip
5. Pay via **Telebirr**, bank transfer, or at a commercial bank (CBE, Awash, Dashen, etc.)

**Option B — In Person at the Sub-City Revenue Office:**
1. Visit nearest Addis Ababa sub-city revenue office
2. Fill in a paper Schedule B declaration form
3. Receive an assessment notice
4. Pay at a designated commercial bank

**Payment Methods:**
- Telebirr (most popular) — via SuperApp or USSD *127#
- Mobile banking (CBE Birr, Awash Mobile)
- Physical CBE or commercial bank branch

> Cash payment directly to the tax office is NOT accepted

---

### When Does a Landlord File? (Declaration Schedule)

- **Annual filing** — once per Ethiopian fiscal year (Hamle 1 to Sene 30 = July 8 to July 7)
- **Deadline:** Within the first few months of the new fiscal year (roughly October/November Ethiopian calendar)
- First-time landlords: register at the tax office within **30 days** of first receiving rental income

---

### Penalties for Non-Payment

| Violation | Penalty |
|-----------|---------|
| Late payment | Interest at **highest commercial bank lending rate + 15%** per annum |
| Late filing / non-declaration | Administrative penalty (Directive 889/2025) |
| Failure to issue tax receipts | **Non-waivable penalty** |
| Systematic tax evasion | Criminal liability — fines or imprisonment |
| Vacant property >6 months | Taxed on imputed rent at market rate |

---

### Compliance Rate — Current Reality

- Historically: **very low** — most Addis Ababa landlords accepted only cash, kept no records, never registered or declared
- 2023–2026: Addis Ababa Revenue Bureau launched aggressive registration campaigns
- ~**500,000 properties** now in the city's digital database — a major improvement
- BUT: informal activity persists driven by high inflation and weak enforcement capacity
- IMF and World Bank reports (2023–2024) flagged Ethiopia's need to expand the rental tax base as essential for fiscal sustainability
- Mandatory banking for rent payments (Proc. 1320/2024) is the key tool to force a paper trail and improve compliance
